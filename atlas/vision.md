# PROJECT ATLAS

> Canonical. This mirrors Adam's vision doc verbatim. Do not rewrite; minor refinements only when
> Adam explicitly asks. Body text in the Google Doc uses Proxima Nova.

Project Atlas is an AI-native fitness ecosystem that combines a high-value consumer fitness app, a
SaaS platform for gyms, and a trusted marketplace connecting people with fitness products, services,
providers, and communities. The platform helps people achieve their goals and become better off
while helping gyms acquire and retain members and enabling fitness businesses to build profitable
customer relationships.

## Infinite problems to solve:

- Gyms need to manage membership (sign up, payments, checkin, etc.)
- Gyms need to drive renewals (reduce/avoid cancelations)
- Fitness providers (gyms, retailers, service providers, food services, etc.) need to find new customers and drive return business
- People want to achieve their fitness goals
- People need help achieving their fitness goals
- People want to learn about new / better products / services / providers that can help them achieve their goals

## Vision

- People achieve their goals and are better off by using Atlas
- Gyms that use the Atlas platform have higher acquisition / retention rates
- Fitness businesses have a profitable relationship with the Atlas platform

## KPIs

- We can quantify that we solve the problems listed above
- UX is measurably better than the alternatives (interaction design, aesthetic, usability, etc.)
- Customer / user ratings come in at 4.9+/5.0, 60+ NPS
- [high initial / continuous adoption rates for both app users as well as Gyms]
- Always profitable: SaaS revenue (from gyms) + commission revenue (from marketplace conversions) cover all operating costs (positive EBITDA)

## Principles

- **Beautifully simple design.** What you do see is captivating, but the majority of what happens is unseen; we believe in the guideline that "the best UI is no UI".
- **Value is obvious.** We focus first on delivering real, measurable, FELT value -- such that it doesn't even need to be said. But we do make it explicit, too :)
- **The most reputable sources.** We don't just grab whatever, we carefully curate the data we leverage, the providers on our marketplace, etc. such that you know you're getting the very best of everything (think Costco)
- **Proven principles.** While we allow for flexibility, configuration, and even customization (where it makes sense), the foundations of every feature are based in time-honored and data/research-backed concepts.
- **High trust.** You know that we've captured everything, you know that your data is secure, you know that you're in very good hands.
- **High ceiling.** While the design is opinionated, and disclosure is progressive / as needed, the capabilities feel endless -- you rarely if ever find that you can't do the exercise, food, configuration, etc. that you want to do. It's all there.
- **Your way.** You don't need to feel forced to interact with others, to choose a product / service, or to employ any specific feature. We have smart defaults and intelligent progressive disclosure, but we always adapt to your wishes.
- **AI where it makes sense.** We don't throw AI at you just because we can; we practice restraint in using the tools available to us, using the right tool for each interaction / job.
- **Bugs.** Assuming you find any (goal is zero bugs discovered by users), you can be assured it will be fixed same day. And in fact, there's a pretty cool way to report a bug -- we're smart enough to catch any unexpected behavior, smartly send it back to the mother ship, and get it resolved. We are known for a bug-free solution.

**10-year strategic target** (the finite destination; distinct from the infinite vision above):
> In 10 years, Atlas is the #1 (in terms of Gym adoption across the US, fitness products/services

**On "goals" vs "better off":** goal achievement is user-defined and finite (not universally
comparable across users); "better off" is a holistic, cross-comparable measure (healthier vitals,
survey responses, business revenue, etc.). Both matter, intentionally.

**On "FELT value":** Adam wants to track that people say they're getting value *without having to
be asked* — a knee-jerk, unsolicited response (interviews, surveys, etc. as possible mechanisms).
