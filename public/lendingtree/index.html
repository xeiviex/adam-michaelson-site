The next chapter

It starts with research. I don't move on ideas until they're rooted in what we learn.

Why research first

Ideas are cheap. Start with focus.

Without context, empathy for the people we serve, and a deliberate, data-backed strategy, a product team becomes a feature factory. Or worse, a game of whack-a-mole.

LendingTree has plenty of ideas, and the roadmap is likely full. Now is the time for some soul searching: deep contextual research that gets us to a valuable, enduring human problem, ideally one tied to the moment money moves, when a loan funds or a policy binds. That's how we'll create an updated product vision and strategy for the next chapter. That is how we'll know which ideas to chase.

My quick take on a few things that are broken

Problems

Three ideas for the Day 60 roadmap. Each starts from a problem I found by shopping LendingTree myself, along with some other light research.

01

Hunted.

We sow distrust and reap sub-optimal economics.

About a week ago, I ran through the experience and was bombarded by calls and texts (see the screenshots to the right), and that continued for days. Even today I received about five outreaches by phone and text. I'm not sure when it will stop, and the decrease may only be because I texted STOP and blocked numbers on my phone.

One click on "View My Rates" is the consent. It covers calls and texts, automated and prerecorded, from LendingTree, its network partners and anyone calling on their behalf, even if the number is on a Do-Not-Call list.1 The shopper never learns who, how many or how often. The model pays for it: up to five match fees can come from a single request (10-K).

Distrust costs money. Shoppers stop answering, so lenders pay for contacts that never connect. The brand loses return visits, and the flood draws lawsuits.2 Trust also moves the numbers: Trustpilot's case study on LendingTree reports up to a 6% lift in conversion from showing reviews on its application pages (source, vendor-reported). The experience that decides whether trust holds is the one after the click.

After going through the LendingTree flow, I was bombarded by texts and phone calls.

1.0/5ConsumerAffairs, 1,584 reviews. Recent reviews describe the calls and texts after one form. 1.05/5Better Business Bureau customer reviews, 95 reviews. Recent reviews describe the same calls and texts. 1.2/5WalletHub, 1,926 reviews. Reviewers say their number was sold on.

Although there are key ratings that look good (like Trustpilot), there are others that reflect this pain. Customers on three independent review sites rate the experience near 1 out of 5, and the reviews name the contact flood. Scores as of October 2026.3

02

One and done.

A transaction with no reason to stay.

The model finishes when the lead is sold. LendingTree is paid when it hands the shopper to a lender or insurer, mostly as match fees (10-K). After the match there is no account to open, no tool to use and no reason to return. The shopper gets no relationship and LendingTree gets no second visit it can count on. Each visit is bought again, at a rising price.4

Consumers are continually shopping, and that's an opportunity for LendingTree. J.D. Power found 53% of auto insurance customers shopped for new coverage in the past year, near the 19-year high of 57% a year earlier (J.D. Power).5 I could not find a published repeat-shopper rate for LendingTree, which could indicate that it's not being tracked and therefore may not be a key behavior-driving metric, which is a problem.

Others made the shopper a member. Credit Karma gave people a free reason to come back and sold to Intuit for about $7.1 billion in 2020 (source). LendingTree tried with credit monitoring: My LendingTree, later the Spring app, signed up 28 million people but did not drive significant revenue.6 Credit monitoring gave no natural reason to return. Insurance does: every policy renews.

77%Of 2025 revenue came from Home and Insurance ($863.6M of $1.12B), mainly match fees recognized when the request is delivered. The money is earned at the handoff, with no reason built in to bring the shopper back. 75%Of Q2 2026 revenue went to selling and marketing ($236.5M of $313.4M). With no returning base, each new visit is bought again. 53%Of auto insurance customers shopped in the past year, near the 19-year high J.D. Power recorded in 2025. Shoppers keep coming back to market, and LendingTree has no relationship to keep them.

Sources: LendingTree 10-K (FY2025) and 10-Q (Q2 2026), J.D. Power (2026 study). Percentages are my arithmetic on reported figures, and the readings beside them are mine. LendingTree publishes no repeat-shopper rate.

03

Into the void.

We send the lead over the wall and rarely learn what happened.

Revenue from mortgage and home equity leads is mostly paid at the match, before anyone knows whether a loan closes (10-K). The lead goes over the wall and, for most leads, LendingTree's view ends there.7

On the far side, lenders learn everything: who answered, who applied, who funded, who vanished. That outcome data is the most valuable data in the chain. Without it, matching runs on proxies, pricing cannot reward quality, and lenders cannot tell a good source from a bad one. Everyone guesses.

A closed loop changes the math. Reported outcomes let LendingTree price on what funds, train matching on results, and show every lender where its best customers come from. The gold is on the other side of the wall.

2.7%Of 2.9 million online mortgage leads studied, about 1 in 37 became a funded loan. A small lift in that rate is a big win for the shopper and the lender alike. $11,909What a lender earns in revenue on one funded mortgage (MBA, Q2 2026). LendingTree is paid a match fee when the lead is sent and shares in none of it. 74%Of loan and insurance applicants in LendingTree's own survey got unwanted calls, texts or email afterward. When no one follows the outcome, the shopper's trust pays the price.

Rapid-impact ideas · What changes

Solutions

Three pilots, each small enough to ship and measure inside six months. They go through the Day 30 assessment like everything else, and the research can kill them.

01 · Choice

The power of choice.

How it works. After the form, the shopper sees clear, comparable options. They pick who contacts them, how and when. An AI guide, building on LendingTree's new AI offer overviews, helps them decide, then follows up by text and email until they choose. Nobody else calls. A shopper who has not chosen after 24 hours goes to their two best matches, shown by name, in place of up to five unnamed lenders.

Why it works. People answer the call they asked for. A chosen lender arrives expected, with context, to a shopper already leaning in. Lenders stop racing four rivals who dial in the same minute. Comparable offers remove the guesswork that breeds distrust. It is the shift LendingTree's CEO called for when trigger leads were banned: competition on "price, service, and product value rather than call volume" (source). Much of the consent work was built in 2024.9

What we expect. Fewer contacts per shopper, and higher answer and funded rates per lead. Lenders pay a premium for a lead the shopper picked.10 The risk is fewer match fees per form, so break-even is the test: if half of shoppers choose, and a fallback lead is worth half of today's, the price per selection needs to be about 1.5 times today's revenue per lead. Everything above that line is upside.

Approach

Scope
An options step with a clear comparison and a choose button, an AI guide inside the experience, and proactive text and email that push toward a choice.
Segment
One Home Equity segment. At least 5,000 leads reaching the options step.Proposed

Three alpha lenders who cover 100% of the segment and at least 80% of its lenders, under a selection-only agreement.

02 · Relationship

A companion.

How it works. A free policy wallet inside the LendingTree app that holds every insurance policy a person has: carrier, coverage, price, renewal date. LendingTree pre-fills what it knows from past quotes, and a policy-import service connects the rest in a tap,11 with upload or email forward as backup. The wallet watches renewals and prices, and prompts a shop at the right moment.

Why it works. Renewal dates are predictable, and a price increase is the top reason people shop.12 A tool that knows the date and the price is there when the decision happens. Useful service earns the next visit, and the next visit costs nothing to acquire.

What we expect. Returning shoppers in place of purchased ones. A lower cost to acquire each quote, more quotes per person over time, and a direct relationship with people who now arrive through an ad. Insurance segment margin was 24% in Q2 2026 against 27% a year earlier. Owned relationships are the lever that moves it. Experian already offers rate monitoring. LendingTree's edge is its quote volume and carrier demand.13

Approach

Scope
A wallet inside the existing LendingTree app and logged-in site. Policies are pre-filled or imported. Auto and home first. A renewal calendar, price checks, and prompts to shop or renew as contracts expire.
Segment
About 2,000 recent insurance quote shoppers who opt in.Proposed
KPIs
Activation (policies connected per user), 90-day retention, quote starts per active user against cold traffic, policies bound, and revenue per member against the cost of buying the same quotes.

03 · Outcomes

Aligned outcomes.

How it works. A simple outcomes API. Partners report what happened to each lead: reached, quoted, applied, bound or funded, declined. Partners who report get better-matched leads and, where the rules allow, pricing tied to results.

Why it works. Incentives drive behavior, and today a partner gains nothing from reporting. With a reward attached, they report. Insurance lead gen already works this way.14 The data trains matching on what converts in place of who clicked, which lifts conversion for partners and the value of every lead.

What we expect. A closed loop. Quality becomes visible by source and by partner, pricing moves toward outcomes in Insurance and Consumer (not mortgage or home equity),15 and a model that learns from results compounds. It also gives Choice the data to keep its offers honest.

Approach

Scope
A small API: report an outcome, read a lead's status, receive a webhook. An incentive for reporting, better-matched leads and outcome-based pricing, and a basic partner dashboard.
Segment
Insurance first, with three alpha auto carriers. Personal loans next.Proposed
KPIs
Reporting coverage (leads with a status inside 14 days), conversion per lead (bound or funded) for matched traffic against control, and cost per bound policy.
Decision
