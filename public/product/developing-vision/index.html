Developing Vision

Something emotionally compelling that you eternally reach for. Your North Star.

The game

Why do we play games? To have fun… by winning. How do we win? By employing a strategy. How do we employ a strategy? By making specific moves.

Vision, strategy, roadmap

  • Vision. Something emotionally compelling that you eternally reach for. Your North Star.
  • Strategy. A framework that you apply to move toward the vision. Your approach.
  • Roadmap. The specific steps you’ll take that follow the strategy, moving us toward the vision. These are specific goals, milestones, projects, tasks, etc.

The infinite quest

Every vision begins with an infinite problem and the infinite quest that answers it.

Infinite problemInjustice · Evil · Misfortune · Imbalance · Gap · Unknown · Itch · Impossibility · Dream
Infinite questJustice · Good · Right a wrong · Bring balance · Close the gap · Discover · Satisfy · Make it possible · Make it reality

Why you need a vision

  • Purpose.
  • Direction.
  • Value.

How to find your vision

  • Understand. Identify the problem. Consider the governing principles and direction. Find out the whole story, the history, and the brutal facts.
  • Feel. Think of those affected by the problem. What is their plight? What is the gap? Consider the magnitude of the opportunity. How can you be an advocate? Where do you stand? Get the drive to overcome.
  • Dream. Imagine a world where the villain has been conquered. Envision the ideal state. Articulate and illustrate that dream in terms of purpose, direction, and value.

Hallmarks of a great vision

  • Infinite. Something that you can always pursue — doesn’t end (otherwise, it’s probably a milestone). Even if you attain it, you have to struggle to keep the state or get back to it.
  • Bold. Something that probably seems almost impossible today. If people don’t think you’re a little audacious, you’re not going far enough.
  • Resonating. Something evocative and compelling. People truly believe in it (especially your team and target market). Tied to human nature.
  • Simple. So understandable that it cannot be misunderstood. Elemental, natural, clear.
  • Aligns with a higher purpose. Whether it be universal morality, human nature, or corporate vision, a great vision is sustained by an overarching principle.

Examples of vision

  • Google: “to provide access to the world’s information in one click.”
  • Apple (under Steve Jobs): “To make a contribution to the world by making tools for the mind that advance humankind.”
  • Amazon: “to be Earth’s most customer-centric company, where customers can find and discover anything they might want to buy online.”
  • Tesla: “To accelerate the world’s transition to sustainable energy.”
  • BambooHR: “Help people and organizations to change for the better.”
